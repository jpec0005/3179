How is sleep affected by a persons lifestyle and what implications does quality of sleep have for their wellbeing in Australia

This visualisation aims to highlight sleep patterns by sex and age group through weekdays, weekends and their average duration. Secondly, it sheds light on lifestyle choices such as amount of hours worked, diet and smoking status to see if they contribute to change in sleep quality. Lastly, how accommodation, vulnerabilities of people such as mental health and the risk of homelessness affects how well people sleep.


Average Sleep Hours and Duration

This section goes over the average onset and waking times for people classified by age group and sex, as well as sleep duration. Please note that hovering over bars/dots, that the minutes are in decimal hours besides axes that are labelled "Hours".



For both sexes, on weekend nights people have a later onset time and people wake up later on weekend mornings. For sleep duration females tend to on average have a longer duration of sleep.



The onset time for the 18-24 age group for both weeknights and weekend nights is more varied between 12am and 1am, compared to the 55-64 age group where the onset time sits around 11pm. Duration for age groups 25-34, 35-44 and 45-54 are similar, this may be influenced by a couple of lifestyle choices covered in the next section.


Lifestyle choices

This section goes over the duration of sleep for hours worked, diet pattern and smoking status, it shows the starting point of when people fall asleep up until they wake up. Please note that hovering over bars, that the minutes are in decimal hours.



People who work more hours go to sleep earlier and wake up earlier where as people not working sleep later and wake up later with a duration roughly around 7 hours and 30 minutes for each group. For diet and smoking there is no substantial difference for duration of sleep. Hours worked has the most effect out of the 3 of determining how well someone sleeps, suggesting that the more hours worked, the more impact it has on your sleep.


SHS Clients and why they sleep rough

This section goes over the Specialist Homelessness Services (SHS), a government-funded organisation in Australia that provides support for people experiencing homelessness or at risk of losing their housing. This is a focus because their clients are labelled as "rough sleepers". They seek help as their accommodation varies and their vulnerabilities that may contribute to poor sleep and over all wellbeing.



Victoria, New South Wales and Queensland have a higher population compared to other states so naturally there will be a large amount of rough sleepers. Trends are stable with a noticable peak for Victoria in 2019. The most prevalant vulnerability these rough sleepers have is mental health, which may contribute to poor sleep but may also be attributed to their situation with being homeless or at risk of homelessness. Accommodation is usually short term with a majority of people not being able to be comfortable for too long at their place of living, this may result in not feeling comfortable enough to get adequate sleep as their accommodation may be temporary.


Conclusion

In summary, females tend to have a longer duration of sleep, onset time for 18-24 year olds are more varied between 12am to 1am where as 55-64 onset sits around 11pm. Hours worked may have a major factor in the duration of sleep, onset and waking time. Lastly, peoples living situation as well as vulnerabilities such as mental health and other at risk factors they might be experiencing. While reading this, if you think that some of these circumstances apply to you, please seek support as poor sleep may hinder your wellbeing.
